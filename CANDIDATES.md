# Candidates — The Vacuum's Alibi: One Line, 200 Characters

All candidates were written from scratch for this bounty. Scoring is
per judge brief, in order: (1) a real reason the vacuum skipped one spot,
(2) genuinely funny on first read, (3) sounds like the machine itself —
deadpan, a little proud of its sensors, schedule and battery,
(4) surprise — a turn or specific detail, not a stock appliance joke,
(5) one tight line, every word earns its place, punch at the end.
Each criterion scored 1–5.

## 1. Bureaucrat — filed and closed (120 chars)
> I filed the dust under 'pre-existing condition' and closed the ticket; my warranty covers floors, not your life choices.

- (1) Reason 5 — the ticket was closed on paperwork, so the spot was skipped.
- (2) Funny 4 — "not your life choices" lands, but the setup is a little long.
- (3) Robot voice 5 — ticket, warranty, filing: pure deadpan machine.
- (4) Surprise 4 — the warranty swerve is good, though bureaucracy is a known angle.
- (5) Tightness 4 — "pre-existing condition" earns its keep; the tail could be shorter.
- **Total: 22**

## 2. Sensor arrogance — the map is never wrong (101 chars)
> My lidar mapped that spot as a permanent rug; I do not argue with my own map, and neither should you.

- (1) Reason 5 — the map says rug, so the vacuum does not clean it.
- (2) Funny 4 — dry and smug; the second clause is the joke.
- (3) Robot voice 5 — lidar, map, refusing to argue: exactly the machine's ego.
- (4) Surprise 4 — "permanent rug" is a specific, unexpected misread.
- (5) Tightness 4 — clean, but the punch is a shrug rather than a snap.
- **Total: 22**

## 3. Alibi — the dust needs a lawyer (101 chars)
> At 14:03 my camera shows me cleaning the hallway; the dust in the corner has no alibi, only a lawyer.

- (1) Reason 5 — timestamped alibi: it was elsewhere, provably.
- (2) Funny 4 — "only a lawyer" is a good twist on the alibi theme.
- (3) Robot voice 4 — camera log is machine-like; the lawyer line leans human.
- (4) Surprise 4 — the alibi flips onto the dust itself.
- (5) Tightness 4 — two balanced clauses, no wasted words.
- **Total: 21**

## 4. Jurisdiction — mop territory (94 chars)

- (2) Funny 4 — "sovereign territory" is the laugh.

- (4) Surprise 3 — jurisdiction is a clever angle, but the wet-rag image is predictable.
- (5) Tightness 4 — tight; "with a wet rag" is doing the work.
- **Total: 21**

## 5. Control sample — science demands a baseline (84 chars)
> I left one crumb as a control group; science demands a baseline, and so does my ego.

- (1) Reason 5 — deliberately left as an experiment, not missed.
- (2) Funny 4 — "and so does my ego" is the actual joke.
- (3) Robot voice 5 — control group, baseline: lab-machine deadpan.
- (4) Surprise 4 — the ego swerve after the science setup.
- (5) Tightness 4 — very short; the middle clause carries a little weight.
- **Total: 22**

## 6. Spot that keeps coming back — reclassified as furniture (100 chars) — WINNER
> I cleaned that spot nine times; it came back ten, so I have officially reclassified it as furniture.

- (1) Reason 5 — it stopped being cleaned because repeated cleaning failed; surrender, stated as policy.
- (2) Funny 5 — nine versus ten is a clean, countable escalation, and "furniture" lands on first read.
- (3) Robot voice 5 — counting attempts, then an official reclassification: deadpan machine logic.
- (4) Surprise 5 — the spot is not dirt any more, it is furniture; the category change is the turn.
- (5) Tightness 5 — every word earns its place; the punch ("furniture") is the last word.
- **Total: 25**

## 10. Alibi witness — the smart fridge (101 chars)

- (3) Robot voice 4 — cross-device confirmation is machine-like, but "home alone" is a human phrase.

- (5) Tightness 4 — balanced, though the second clause softens the punch.
